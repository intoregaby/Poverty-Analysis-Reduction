# User Vision Translated into Functional Rules

## Vocabulary

- **VUP** = Vision Umurenge Programme (not “VIP”; earlier spoken name interpreted as VUP from project documentation).
- **Programme / programme owner** = a government programme or NGO that funds support and/or recruits field agents.
- **Programme member** = an enrolled beneficiary/household supported under an approved programme; this is NOT the same as an agent.
- **Field agent** = a recruited and paid worker (often a job seeker who completed secondary school/university) who has been evaluated and assigned to help programme members; not simply a statistical data enumerator.
- **Practical project** = an actual implemented livelihood/business activity, not attendance at a lesson.
- **Field evidence** = consented, context-labelled record (photo optional, documents, activity receipts, validation notes) supporting a milestone, not a proof of increased income on its own.

## Business opportunity chain

1. A programme registers a budget and funding obligations; identifies eligible communities and vacancies.
2. Job seekers apply to agent roles. Assess **practical skills and knowledge**, ability to teach, business experience or capability, safeguarding, digital literacy, communication, community language, and location. Accept certificates, demonstrations and supervised training rather than unjustifiably demanding university credentials.
3. Human recruiter interviews, assesses and appoints agents under clear terms and lawful compensation. Employment eligibility and age requirements need local legal review; do not assume every secondary graduate is an adult.
4. Programme manager assigns verified members to a trained agent respecting geography, workload, conflicts of interest and member consent.
5. Agent first understands household capabilities/resources/preferences; prepares an achievable plan with the member, selects relevant skills lessons and trains them.
6. Agent **assists implementation**: e.g. helps a member produce a plan, source inputs, register business where required, start an agricultural, service or cooperative activity, learn pricing/bookkeeping/customer service and test an actual sale.
7. Agent logs activities and milestones. Photos must be purpose-limited, consented, privately stored, with metadata and review; no need to photograph vulnerable people or children. Alternative evidence allowed.
8. Supervisor verifies that an activity happened; a different source is required for claims of revenue or financial benefit. Record revenue, cost, profit and date only when voluntarily reported and supported; “income improved” requires an appropriate baseline/follow-up method, not one photo.
9. A member receives support transfers when actually confirmed by authorized financial rails. Bank/mobile-money enrollment is offered with guidance; document accessible alternative support paths.
10. Government sees programme funding, approved allocations, transfers with statuses, agent compensation obligations, validated trainings/projects, aggregated coverage, complaints and monitoring results. Analysts use official NISR evidence to identify needs; governments act through permitted workflows.

## Funding questions government must answer

- Where did approved funding originate (source organisation/investor, agreement, restriction, period, grant vs investment)?
- How much was budgeted, committed, approved for disbursement, sent to provider, acknowledged, settled, returned, spent on agents and still available?
- How many members were assigned, received scheduled payments, were trained, implemented business activities, and independently validated progress?
- What is missing, disputed, outstanding, delayed, duplicated or unverified by source/location/programme/period?
- What financing and programme performance data is available versus unavailable? **Do not pretend all external spending is monitored.**

## Key logical distinctions

- **Grant vs investment vs loan**: different rights and obligations; avoid treating all inflows as spendable grants.
- **Programme payment vs agent wage**: separate recipient, approval, liability and ledger dimensions.
- **Transferred vs received**: a payment request is not evidence of successful settlement.
- **Account opened vs financially included**: account availability, activation, practical use and control are different indicators.
- **Loan referral vs loan approval**: lender alone decides credit eligibility/terms; platform records referral and consent.
- **Training attended vs skill demonstrated vs livelihood implemented vs income verified**: four distinct outcomes, never one metric.
- **Government oversight vs unrestricted surveillance**: legal mandate, role scope, aggregated reporting and privacy safeguards.

## Non-negotiable ethical constraints

- Accessible assistance for people without phones, literacy or ID; define lawful alternative pathways instead of conditioning social support on digital or bank participation without authority.
- Financial advice must not pressure vulnerable families into debt. Display loan terms, repayment obligations and risk clearly; no promise of approval.
- Family photos, location traces, financial health and beneficiary status are sensitive. Collect the minimum with lawful basis, informed consent where required, retention, access/correction options and escalation.
- Aid eligibility and rights are decided by authorised human programme officers under published programme rules; scores may inform review, not determine an adverse outcome.
- Reports show source lineage, denominators, observed vs demo vs modeled status and limitations.
